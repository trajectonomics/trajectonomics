# The Estate page reads what heirs would receive, and writes the estate block

"What would my family receive?" follows "how much can I spend?". `traj` states it ([cli ADR 0064](../../../cli/docs/adr/0064-the-estate-block-is-the-callers-and-projection-run-estate-states-what-heirs-receive.md)): everything owned, less what is owed, less the settlement costs, less the gifts, on each roll-up end. The page reads those tables, writes the person's own costs and gifts as the document's `estate` block, and tries another block in a What-if session. Every figure is the CLI's text ([ADR 0002](0002-the-cli-is-the-only-source-of-figures.md)); no share, amount, cost or gift is a default anywhere in the page, and no tax is worked out.

## Decision

- **Reads.** Every `projection run` of the Snapshot and of a what-if takes `--estate`, so the `estate` and `estateLines` tables are in the run it already reads (a plan with no block still gets them, with `costs` and `gifts` at 0.00). No new read leaf, so `leaves.test.ts` and the CLI's dashboard stub are unchanged.
- **The date.** The page reads the `estate` row on the chosen date, or the first roll-up end after it, and the last row when the date is past the plan's last year (`rowOn`). The default is the later life expectancy of the household's holder and partner (a dependent never moves it): for each, the first annual row whose `ages` entry reaches their `lifeExpectancy`, so the page compares the CLI's integers and its date text and computes no age or date. With one adult it is theirs. When the plan ends before that, or the household names no adult, the plan's last year is shown, and the page says why in a sentence. The other choices are each adult's own, every reached Milestone, every household anchor on a Flow's start or end (its date is the one the document holds), and the plan's last year. The choice is the address bar's `at`.
- **Terms.** The engine states only `net` and `toHeirs` in today's money (`realNet`, `realToHeirs`). With "Today's money" the page shows those two in today's money and every other line nominal, each tagged "Nominal"; with "Both" the two have today's money beside them. Debts and costs are separate lines, because "together" would need an addition in the page; the breakdown puts each cost and gift under the engine's own total. The diagram and the Bands are nominal.
- **A negative net** is stated as it is: heirs and gifts are 0.00, the page says so in a sentence with the shortfall (the net with its sign taken off, as text), and the flow diagram is not drawn, because its uses would exceed its source.
- **The form** edits the whole block in the right-side drawer: costs and gifts as lists in the order taken, a line added, moved up or down, or removed. A line is a share (a percent with at most two places, a rounding, and for a cost its base: everything owned or chosen asset Accounts, a home included; a gift's is the net estate) or a fixed amount in major units that grows by inflation or a percent a year. No share, amount, base, rounding or growth is filled in: each starts unchosen and an unchosen one is a problem at its field. The form says every line is the person's own and that inheritance tax is not worked out and can be typed as a cost. A share is written as `traj --decimal` reads it, a percent string such as `"1.00"`, and a fixed amount in major units; a fixed line's growth keeps its integer `bps`. A new line is named by the person (its id is that name as a slug); an existing line keeps its id. No line left saves no `estate` key, so the plan is as it was before the first line.
- **Saving** is one `apply-plan` Edit naming the hash the page read ([ADR 0008](0008-the-dashboard-edits-scenarios-through-traj-on-loopback.md)). A refusal lands on the field its path names, in the CLI's own sentence. The path comes in two forms: the core's refusals name a line by its index (`document.estate.costs.0.bps`), while the decimal conversion names it by its id (`document.estate.costs.settlement.bps`); the mapping (`refusal.ts`) matches the segment against the saved block's ids first and its indexes second, so both land on the same field. A line the form did not send, or a path outside the block, is shown for the whole form.
- **Try as a what-if** ([ADR 0010](0010-the-what-if-session-reads-through-traj-on-loopback.md), amended). The block is tried as one Set of the whole object, `--set estate=<json>` (`withTriedEstate`), because a Set of a field cannot add or remove a line; a later try replaces the earlier one, and the session bar words it "Estate lines tried as edited" with no share or amount. `traj` replaces a path the plan holds and refuses `estate` on a plan that has no block (`No key or id "estate" under the document`), so the form offers the try only when the plan already states a block, and says to save a first line otherwise; trying "no lines" is a Set of `{}`. The page then shows the what-if's headline, diagram and line above the saved plan's.
- **Navigation and Home.** Estate is `/estate/`, in its own group after Optimize, listed when the selected plan has a document. The Home card "Your heirs would receive" states what heirs receive on the default date, says which date that is and whether the plan states a settlement cost, and links to the page.
- **The Demo.** `base` of the Demo has an estate block (1% of everything owned, court and legal fees of 50,000.00 that keep pace with inflation, and a gift of 5% of what is left, each rounding half up or a fixed amount); `retire-early` has none, so the page shows both states. Every share and amount is invented. Adding `--estate` to every run took the snapshot to 1,059,691 bytes, over Biome's 1 MiB formatter limit, so the Demo's horizon is 31 December 2053 and not 2054 (one year less on every read; the Withdrawals page keeps 4 rule years and its bands move). The snapshot is 1,040,886 bytes. Pinned figures that moved: the lowest year of the Bands (835,913.64, 882,584.51, 963,414.26, 1,024,160.36 and 1,090,453.31), the horizon on the Chance and Health pages, and the FI date of `base`, now Oct 2040 (it was Jul 2041, because the survival method has one year less to survive). The default date of the Demo is beyond its horizon (the household's two adults both reach their life expectancy in 2080), so the page shows the plan's last year and says why.

## Considered Options

- **The later life expectancy of everyone in the household.** Rejected: a child's life expectancy would move the date to a century away, and an estate passes from its adults.
- **A Set of each changed line field.** Rejected: a Set replaces a path that exists and cannot add or remove a line, and a line's path would need its id.
- **Showing every line in today's money.** Rejected: the engine states only two, and a page that scaled the rest would do arithmetic on money (ADR 0002). The engine can state the rest (#393).
- **Debts and costs as one line.** Rejected for the same reason.

## Consequences

- A change in the CLI's output fails `src/lib/contract/projection-run.test.ts` (its golden) and `scripts/estate.test.ts`, which works one estate row by hand against the real binary: 1% of 1,391,400.00 is 13,914.00, the net is 1,391,400.00 less 184,000.00 of debts and 13,914.00, which is 1,193,486.00, 5% of that is 59,674.30, and heirs receive 1,133,811.70.
- `scripts/estate-refusals.test.ts` pins the refusal paths in both forms against the real binary, and `scripts/estate-try.test.ts` pins the whole-block Set and `traj`'s refusal of it on a plan with no block.
- A `traj` older than `projection run --estate` refuses it as an unknown flag, which fails the Snapshot's run reads; the CLI the Dashboard ships with has it.
